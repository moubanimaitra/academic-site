About

Moubani Maitra

My research sits at the intersection of critical political economy, the anthropology of the state, and citizen-state relations in South Asia. I draw on long-term ethnographic fieldwork carried out over several years to understand how citizenship has evolved in contemporary India — tracing this evolution from the Congress-led United Progressive Alliance to the BJP-led National Democratic Alliance, and asking how authoritarianism and neoliberalism manifest at the grassroots level.

My analysis focuses on the structural and institutional conditions that allow such authoritarianism not only to emerge and operate, but also to sustain itself over time.

PhD Project

Contesting Democracy: Liberalisation, Institutions, and the State in Uttar Pradesh

This dissertation examines shifts in India's political economy following the Bharatiya Janata Party's 2014 election victory, tracing this transformation through four major themes: the shrinking of the public sector through new forms of privatisation and state-business nexus, the reconfiguration of civil society, evictions and land dispossession, and the neoliberal welfare regime.

Taken together, these four themes demonstrate how the gap between formal and substantive democracy widens as contemporary manifestations of neoliberalism — through outsourcing and privatisation, the expansion of private capital, the erosion of labour rights, and the digitalisation of welfare schemes — intersect with authoritarian tendencies such as the centralisation of power, the weakening of due process, repression of civil society, and the erosion of accountability mechanisms. Collectively, these developments characterise a democratic decline under the BJP in India. I argue that democratic backsliding is often accomplished through seemingly ordinary legal operations of institutional power, rather than through overt rule-breaking or co-optation. But this requires alignment across multiple state actors and institutions, whose combined action creates conditions where erosion proceeds with minimal accountability.

Courses Taught

Critical Perspectives on Urban India

Course Curator & Lecturer · Fall 2026

Course Description

This course offers a critical, ethnographically grounded introduction to urban India, examining the social, political, and economic dynamics that produce inequality and exclusion in Indian cities. Key themes include the formal-informal divide, informal settlements, land rights and housing, precarious labour and the gig economy, welfare politics, the urban middle class, and questions of environment, gender, and citizenship. Drawing on case studies from Delhi, Mumbai, Hyderabad, and Bengaluru, the course situates these debates within specific political economy contexts while enabling comparative insight across urban India. It is suited to students in urban studies, anthropology, sociology, development studies, and social policy.
